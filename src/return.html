Make your organisation’s data asset return

Four parts and twelve steps, from getting ready to sending your list to GDS (the Government Digital Service). Each step says who does it, what you need and what you end up with.

When: GDS plans to collect the first return, called the Baseline Return, in January 2027. Aim to be ready by Friday 18 December 2026, before the Christmas break.

The January date comes from the programme plan. It is not yet written in the published policy, so check with us before you rely on it.

Help: use our contact form.

The twelve steps

Get ready

  1. 1. Name who is in charge

    Your takes charge of the return. They name a coordinator and give them time to do the work.

    Who does this
    Your chief data officer leads. This is the most senior person in charge of data. The coordinator does the day-to-day work.
    What you need

    Your organisation chart, and a list of the organisations that report to your department (often called ).

    What you end up with
    A person in charge, a coordinator, and a list of everyone who needs to take part.

    Based on: Data Asset Management Policy (opens GOV.UK)

    Next: 2. Agree who owns your data →

  2. 2. Agree who owns your data

    Agree how you will decide who owns each set of data. Then take it to your board (the senior group that runs your organisation).

    Who does this
    Your chief data officer, with your senior information risk owner (the person responsible for information risk).
    What you need

    The data ownership model (opens GOV.UK).

    What you end up with
    An agreed way of naming owners. Where two people seem responsible for the same data, one of them is chosen.

    Based on: Data ownership model (opens GOV.UK)

    Next: 3. Start a list of possible assets →

Make your list

  1. 3. Start a list of possible assets

    Gather what you already know about. You do not need to start from nothing.

    Who does this
    Your coordinator.
    What you need

    Use any lists you already have. GDS has made a starter list for each group of organisations: ask us for yours. You may also have a list of your information, a or data sharing agreements. Include data you get from others, and data that suppliers hold for you.

    What you end up with
    A list of possible assets to test in the next step.

    Based on: Identifying and recording critical data assets (opens GOV.UK)

    Next: 4. Check each one against the scope principles →

  2. 4. Check each one against the scope principles

    An asset goes in the return only if it is OFFICIAL and all four DAM Policy scope principles apply. Write down your answer to each one. The asset test walks you through them.

    Who does this
    Your coordinator, with the people who know each set of data.
    What you need

    First, it is OFFICIAL. OFFICIAL with markings such as sensitive counts. SECRET and TOP SECRET do not go in.

    Then the four principles:

    1. It meets a critical criterion. It supports at least one of seven critical purposes, numbered 1 to 7:
      1. delivering public services
      2. a legal, legislative or contractual requirement
      3. defence, national security and resilience
      4. developing and evaluating policy
      5. official statistics
      6. the public interest
      7. fraud and error
    2. It is shared. It has value across government through reuse: it is shared with a public sector organisation under a data sharing agreement, published as open data, or could create value if shared. For the Baseline Return, start with assets that are already shared or published.
    3. You are accountable for it. Your organisation manages or controls it. Data from someone else counts only if you have materially changed or enriched it.
    4. It is the source. If an extract, report, API or other product is critical, report the source data behind it, even if the source itself is never shared. Report a product separately only if it is managed and owned in its own right.

    For official statistics, include the data behind them (the administrative data, surveys and censuses), not the published figures.

    What you end up with
    An answer to each test, for each possible asset. Anything with a “no” goes on your list of assets left out, with the reason. In the workbook, the security marking (column M) shows the OFFICIAL check and the critical purpose numbers (column J) show the first principle.

    Based on: Identifying and recording critical data assets (opens GOV.UK), Data Asset Management Policy (opens GOV.UK) and the GDS DAM Policy FAQs (November 2026).

    Next: 5. Agree the list across your organisation →

  3. 5. Agree the list across your organisation

    Check the list with each part of your organisation.

    Who does this
    Your coordinator, with your (the person who makes sure personal data is handled lawfully).
    What you need

    The list from step 4.

    What you end up with
    An agreed list. Your data protection officer confirms that listing an asset does not create any new sharing or use of the data.

    Based on: Data Asset Management Policy (opens GOV.UK)

    Next: 6. Name an owner and a steward for each asset →

  4. 6. Name an owner and a steward for each asset

    Each asset needs an owner and a . The owner agrees in writing. The steward looks after the data day to day.

    Who does this
    Data owners, with your coordinator.
    What you need

    Who each asset goes to and comes from.

    What you end up with
    An owner and a steward for each asset. Keep their names in your own records. The return only asks whether each one is assigned, Yes or No. It does not record names or email addresses.

    Based on: Data ownership model (opens GOV.UK)

    Next: 7. Fill in the 24 details →

  5. 7. Fill in the 24 details

    Describe each asset in plain English in the version 3 Baseline Return workbook. It asks for 24 details. Some must always be filled in, some only when they apply.

    Who does this
    Stewards, and whoever manages your data catalogue.
    What you need

    Your data catalogue, if you have one. This is a list that describes your data. You can copy details from it rather than typing them again.

    What you end up with
    The 24 details for each asset. Where the workbook has a list of answers, choose from it. See all 24 details.

    Based on: Recording metadata to describe critical data assets (opens GOV.UK) and the version 3 Baseline Return workbook.

    Next: 8. Link a data quality plan →

  6. 8. Link a data quality plan

    Each asset needs a plan to keep its data good enough to use. It also needs a date to look at it again.

    Who does this
    Data owners.
    What you need

    The data quality action plan guide (opens GOV.UK). You may already have tools, processes or resources you can use.

    What you end up with
    The status of each asset’s quality plan (column T) and, if you have it, where the evidence is kept (column U). A gap is accepted, but the asset is not counted as ready until a plan is in place.

    Based on: Data quality action plan guide (opens GOV.UK)

    Next: 9. Check the returns from organisations that report to you →

Check and send

  1. 9. Check the returns from organisations that report to you

    Departments only: check what the organisations that report to you have done before everything goes to GDS.

    Who does this
    Your department’s coordinator.
    What you need

    The returns from each organisation that reports to your department.

    What you end up with
    One view of what is ready, what has gaps and what still needs fixing.

    Based on: Data Asset Management Policy (opens GOV.UK)

    Next: 10. Sign off and send →

  2. 10. Sign off and send

    Your chief data officer signs off the return. Your coordinator fills in the Baseline Return workbook (version 3) and emails it to the team.

    Who does this
    Your chief data officer and your coordinator.
    What you need

    Your finished list, and the version 3 workbook the team sends you. Use the list check below to find common problems first.

    What you end up with
    A signed-off return, emailed to the team’s shared mailbox. The team gives you the address. You will get a reference number for your return.

    Based on: Data Asset Management Policy (opens GOV.UK)

    Next: 11. Keep it current →

Keep it up to date

  1. 11. Keep it current

    Review your list and its details at least once a year.

    Who does this
    Data owners and stewards.
    What you need

    This year’s return.

    What you end up with
    An up-to-date list. The workbook does not record the review, so note the date in your own records. Next year, start from this year’s list rather than from nothing.

    Based on: Data Asset Management Policy (opens GOV.UK)

    Next: 12. Share what you learned →

  2. 12. Share what you learned

    Tell GDS how it went and pass on what worked to other organisations.

    Who does this
    Your chief data officer and coordinator.
    What you need

    Your notes on what was hard and what helped.

    What you end up with
    Lessons that make the next return easier for everyone.

    Based on: Data Asset Management Policy (opens GOV.UK)

Is my list ready to send?

This finds the most common problems, so your return can be accepted first time.

The check follows the version 3 workbook. First save the workbook’s return sheet as a CSV file.

The file check needs JavaScript, which is switched off or blocked in your browser. You can still use the list of checks below to go through your file by hand.

Files are read by your own browser. Nothing is uploaded or sent anywhere.

What the check looks for

Must fix

  • The file has all 24 columns of the version 3 template.
  • The 13 details that must be filled in are filled in.
  • Text fits the length the template allows.
  • Yes or No questions say Yes or No.
  • The security marking is OFFICIAL or OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE. SECRET and TOP SECRET data does not go in.
  • Critical purposes are numbers from 1 to 7, each used once.
  • Answers chosen from a list use one of the list’s values, where we have the list.
  • An asset recorded “In catalogue” gives its catalogue reference.
  • Geospatial standards come from the list, and “Not applicable” stands alone.
  • No asset is marked “Out of scope”.
  • There are no email addresses, in the return or in your list of assets left out. The return does not record people’s names or contact details.
  • The template’s worked example has been deleted.
  • No quote mark is left open, which would join rows together.

We will ask

  • An identifier used more than once, written in any mix of capitals. This is allowed only for an approved version or licence variant.
  • In your list of assets left out (our suggestion): which test each one failed, and why.

Policy gap These are accepted. They are not mistakes: they show us where to help.

  • No data owner or no data steward assigned yet.
  • The asset is not in a data catalogue yet.
  • A gap in data quality assurance.

Tips

  • Recommended details are filled in: sharing status, version and discoverability.
  • The security marking is written exactly as OFFICIAL or OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE.
  • Asset names spell out abbreviations, and no two assets have almost the same name.

What the check cannot do: it cannot tell whether a detail marked “Fill in if it applies” applies to your asset. For sharing status, access rights, licence, quality status and discoverability, it checks that you answered, but not your answer, because we do not yet have the workbook’s full lists.

The 24 details, and a blank template

The return is the version 3 Baseline Return workbook, with one row for each asset. The team sends you the workbook. To use the check on this page, save its return sheet as a CSV file, or start from the blank CSV template below, which has the same 24 headings. If you leave any assets out, we suggest listing them in a second file with the test each one failed and why. This list is our suggestion, not part of the workbook. Write roles, such as “data owner”, not people’s names.

Download the blank list template (CSV) Download the blank template for assets left out (CSV)

Keep the heading row of each template exactly as it is.

The 24 details, in the workbook’s order (columns A to X), in seven groups. 13 must be filled in, 5 when they apply, 3 are recommended and 3 are optional.

Show:

Asset identity

  • Column A Unique identifier Must be filled in

    Your own reference for the asset. Up to 100 characters. Use it once, unless the row is an approved version or licence variant.

  • Column B Data asset name Must be filled in

    A short name people will recognise. Spell out abbreviations. Up to 200 characters.

  • Column C Description Must be filled in

    What the data is, in plain English. Between 20 and 2,000 characters.

Organisation and provenance

  • Column D Owning department / publisher Must be filled in

    The one organisation accountable for the asset. Up to 200 characters.

  • Column E ALB / agency Fill in if it applies

    The arm’s length body or agency, if there is one. Up to 200 characters.

  • Column F Source / provenance Fill in if it applies

    Where the data comes from. You can summarise several sources in one statement. Up to 1,000 characters.

Accountability

  • Column G Data owner assigned? Must be filled in

    Yes or No. Do not write the owner’s name. “No” is accepted and recorded as a policy gap.

  • Column H Data steward assigned? Must be filled in

    Yes or No. Do not write the steward’s name. “No” is accepted and recorded as a policy gap.

  • Column I Stewardship model Optional

    Central, Federated, Distributed, Shared service or Other.

Scope and classification

  • Column J Critical purpose number(s) Must be filled in

    The numbers of the purposes it serves, from 1 to 7, separated by commas, for example “1, 4”. Use the numbers from the workbook’s list.

  • Column K Primary sharing status Recommended

    Choose from the workbook’s list. An asset marked “Out of scope” does not go in the return.

  • Column L Legal gateway / regulatory basis Fill in if it applies

    The law or rule that allows the sharing, if there is one. You can summarise several. Up to 500 characters.

  • Column M Security classification / marking Must be filled in

    OFFICIAL or OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE, written exactly like that. SECRET and TOP SECRET data does not go in.

  • Column N Contains personal data? Must be filled in

    Yes or No.

  • Column O Source-asset access rights Must be filled in

    Choose one category from the workbook’s list.

Licence and version

  • Column P Primary licence / usage basis Must be filled in

    Choose from the workbook’s list. If no one basis is the main one, choose “Multiple / varies”.

  • Column Q Asset version / update status Recommended

    The current version, or how often it is updated. Up to 100 characters.

Data management

  • Column R Metadata recorded in catalogue? Must be filled in

    In catalogue, Planned or Not in catalogue. “Not in catalogue” is accepted and recorded as a policy gap.

  • Column S Catalogue / metadata reference Fill in if it applies

    The catalogue reference. Needed when the answer to R is “In catalogue”. Up to 250 characters.

  • Column T DQ assurance / action plan status Must be filled in

    Choose from the workbook’s list. A “Gap” is accepted, but the asset is not counted as ready until a quality plan is in place. The check looks for an answer that starts with the word “Gap”.

  • Column U Quality evidence reference Optional

    Where the evidence of quality checks is kept. Up to 250 characters.

  • Column V Discoverability / interoperability status Recommended

    Choose from the workbook’s list.

Standards and continuity

  • Column W Geospatial standard applied Fill in if it applies

    UPRN (Unique Property Reference Number), USRN (Unique Street Reference Number), GEMINI (the UK standard for describing geographic data), Other or Not applicable. You can list several, separated by commas. “Not applicable” goes on its own.

  • Column X Previous ESDA return reference Optional

    Your reference from the earlier Essential Shared Data Assets (ESDA) return, if the asset was in it. Up to 100 characters.

Based on: Recording metadata to describe critical data assets (opens GOV.UK) and the version 3 Baseline Return workbook.

What happens after I send it?

  1. We receive it

    You get a reference number. We keep your original file exactly as you sent it.

  2. We check it

    Automatic checks first, then a person looks at anything unclear.

  3. We ask once, if we need to

    If something needs changing, we send one message listing everything.

  4. It joins the register

    Your assets join one cross-government register, with a record of where each came from.

  5. We use it to help

    The register shows where support is needed most.

An honest gap is not a mistake. If your return says, for example, that an asset has no data quality plan yet, we accept it. It shows us where to offer support. We only ask you to change things that are wrong or unclear.

What do the words mean?

Show the words and what they mean
Critical data asset
A set of data that goes to, or comes from, another part of government, and that important work depends on.
Baseline Return
The first list of critical data assets each organisation sends to GDS.
OFFICIAL
The lowest level of the government’s security classifications. Most government data is OFFICIAL.
Data owner
The person who is responsible for a set of data and decides how it is used.
Data steward
The person who looks after a set of data day to day.
Arm’s length body
An organisation that is part of government but works at a distance from its department, such as an agency.
Data quality action plan
A plan for keeping a set of data accurate and complete enough to use.
CSV file
A simple file for table data that any spreadsheet program can open and save.
DAM
Data Asset Management, the government policy this return is part of.
Data catalogue
A list that describes the data an organisation holds, so people can find it.
Data protection officer
The person in an organisation who makes sure personal data is handled lawfully.
Policy gap
Something the policy expects that is not in place yet, such as an asset with no data owner. A policy gap is not a mistake. We accept it and use it to decide where to help.
ESDA
Essential Shared Data Assets, an earlier government list of shared data. Column X asks for your reference from it.
GDS
The Government Digital Service, which runs the Data Asset Management programme.

How this site was made, and what it does with your files

Your files stay with you. When you check a file, it is read by your own browser. Nothing is uploaded, stored or sent anywhere. There are no cookies and no tracking.
