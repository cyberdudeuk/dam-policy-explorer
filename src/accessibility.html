Accessibility and inclusion

We want everyone who needs this site to be able to use it, whatever their abilities, background or equipment.

What we have done

  • Plain English, with short sentences and no unexplained jargon. Words that might be new are explained where they appear: select a dotted word to see what it means.
  • Only the essentials are shown first. More detail opens when you choose it.
  • You can use the whole site with a keyboard, and you can always see where you are on the page. On the process map, the arrow keys follow the arrows.
  • It is built to work with screen readers and voice control: every control has a clear, visible label, and its name matches what you see. Every picture or map has a text version.
  • Explanations that pop up stay open while you move onto them, and close with the Escape key.
  • You can zoom to 400% and use it on a small phone screen without the page scrolling sideways. The process map can scroll within its own box.
  • It follows your device’s light or dark setting, and its colours meet contrast standards in both. Colour is never the only way something is shown: shapes, borders and words carry the same meaning.
  • Animation is kept to a minimum and stops if your device asks for reduced motion.
  • Pages can be printed. If JavaScript is on, all hidden sections open when you print.
  • Everything you read works without JavaScript. Only the file check and the drawn map need it, and both have a text alternative.
  • There is no tracking, and files you check never leave your device.

How we check it

Every change is tested automatically with a tool called axe, against the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA. This runs at three screen widths and in light and dark mode. The tests also check the pages with a keyboard, without JavaScript, and for plain English.

Automated tools find only some accessibility problems. That is why we also need people to test it (see below).

Compliance status

This site is partially compliant with WCAG 2.2 at level AA. Automated tests find no failures, but it has not yet been tested by disabled people or audited by an accessibility specialist.

What we know is not yet good enough

  • The site has not yet been tested by people who use assistive technology, such as screen readers or switch access. We plan to do this before the site leaves its prototype stage.
  • The site is in English only. Please ask us if you need it in Welsh or another language.
  • The file check reads CSV files only. If you cannot save your list as CSV, tell us through the contact form and we will help.
  • Our contact form is on GitHub, which needs an account. If you cannot use it, ask a colleague who can, or your department’s coordinator, to contact us for you.

Tell us about a problem

If something does not work for you, or you need the information in another format, such as large print, audio, Easy Read or braille, use our contact form. Tell us what you need and how to reach you. Do not include personal or sensitive information, because the form can be seen by others.

If you are not happy with our response, you can contact the Equality Advisory and Support Service.

How this site was made, and what it does with your files

Your files stay with you. When you check a file, it is read by your own browser. Nothing is uploaded, stored or sent anywhere. There are no cookies and no tracking.

Where the content comes from. Facts about the policy come from the published Data Asset Management Policy and its guidance on GOV.UK. How the team processes returns is the team’s own proposed design, and the page says so. The full list of sources is in our sources list.

How it was made. Made for the GDS Government Data directorate - Data Quality & Management team with the help of an AI assistant (Claude, made by Anthropic), which also drew the icons. Facts about the policy and its guidance were checked against the published pages on GOV.UK. The team is responsible for the content. Content last checked: 13 November 2026.

No guarantee. This is a prototype. If anything here disagrees with the published guidance on GOV.UK, the guidance is right. Please tell us so we can fix it.

Contact. We use a contact form so that messages reach whoever is on duty. The form is on GitHub and what you write there can be seen by others, so do not include personal or sensitive information.
