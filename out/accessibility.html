Accessibility and inclusion

We want everyone to be able to use this app, whatever their needs, device or way of working.

Using this app

  • Change colours (including high contrast), text size, spacing and animation in Display settings at the top of every page.
  • Zoom up to 400% without anything being cut off or needing sideways scrolling.
  • Use the whole app with a keyboard, a screen reader, voice control or a switch.
  • Read every page with JavaScript turned off. Only the interactive tools need it.
  • Point at, or tab to, any word with a dotted underline to see what it means.
  • Print any page: hidden sections open when you print.

Inclusion

  • We write in plain English, test our pages for reading age, and explain every specialist term.
  • We make no assumptions about where people work, their size of organisation or their maturity with data. Small arm's length bodies are as much our users as large departments.
  • Answers do not depend on knowing who to ask. Everything we tell one coordinator, we publish for all.
  • The app works on slow connections and older devices: there are no web fonts, no tracking and no large downloads.

How accessible it is

This app aims to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines version 2.2 (opens in a new tab) to level AA. Every build is checked with an automated tool (axe-core) at three screen widths and in light, dark and high contrast. Automated tools find only some problems, so we will also test with people who use assistive technology.

Known issues

  • The contact form is on GitHub, which we do not control. If it does not work for you, ask your organisation's DAM Policy coordinator to pass your message on.
  • Links to GOV.UK open in a new tab, because the app may be running inside Microsoft Teams. Each such link says so.
  • We have not yet tested with users of assistive technology. This is on our roadmap.

Tell us about a problem

If something does not work for you, use our contact form (opens in a new tab). Tell us the page and what happened. We aim to reply within 5 working days.

Enforcement

The Equality and Human Rights Commission is responsible for enforcing the Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) (No. 2) Accessibility Regulations 2018. If you are not happy with how we respond to your complaint, contact the Equality Advisory and Support Service (opens in a new tab).
