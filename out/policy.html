The policy in plain English

The Data Asset Management Policy asks government organisations to find, describe and look after the data they share with each other. GDS published it on 27 May 2026.

This page explains the policy. It does not replace it. Read the policy on GOV.UK (opens in a new tab).

Why it exists

Government relies on data that passes between organisations. When nobody knows what that data is, who owns it or how good it is, services and decisions suffer. The policy sets a minimum standard for quality, discoverability, interoperability and clear ownership. Policy

What you must do

  • Find and record your data assets

    Identify the data assets in scope, manage metadata about them in your data catalogue, and report them to GDS.

    Section 4 of the policy Policy

  • Departments coordinate their arm's length bodies

    Send metadata on your own assets, and coordinate and assure the returns from your arm's length bodies.

    Section 4 of the policy Policy

  • Name an owner for every asset

    Every identified data asset must have a named data owner, accountable for decisions about it.

    Section 5 of the policy Policy

  • Plan for quality

    Every identified data asset must have a data quality action plan or equivalent.

    Section 6 of the policy Policy

  • Keep it up to date

    Maintain what you send and review it every year.

    Section 4 of the policy Policy

  • Your chief data officer is responsible

    The chief data officer of each organisation in scope is responsible for putting the policy into practice there.

    Section 8 of the policy Policy

What counts as a critical data asset

First, the data must be OFFICIAL. SECRET and TOP SECRET data is not in scope. Policy

Then all four DAM Policy scope principles must apply. GDS position

  1. It meets a critical criterion. It supports at least one of seven critical purposes.
  2. It is shared. It has value across government through reuse: shared under a data sharing agreement, published as open data, or could create value if shared.
  3. You are accountable for it. You manage or control it, including others' data you have materially changed or enriched.
  4. It is the source. Report the source data behind a critical extract, report or API.
The seven critical purposes
  1. Delivering public services
  2. A legislative, legal or contractual requirement
  3. Defence, national security and resilience
  4. Developing and evaluating policy
  5. Official statistics
  6. The public interest
  7. Fraud and error

What the policy does not do

  • It does not make you share data.
  • It does not remove any legal obligation you already have.
  • It does not promise that data will be available, or of a given quality, beyond what is already agreed.

Source: Data Asset Management Policy in government (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab)

Myths and facts

  • Myth Listing data means we have to share it.

    Fact No. The policy does not create a duty to share data or remove any legal obligation. Policy

  • Myth We have to report every data asset we hold.

    Fact No. Only critical data assets, as the policy defines them, go in the return. GDS position

  • Myth Data owners replace information asset owners.

    Fact No. Information asset owners keep their role. The data owner role adds to it. Policy

  • Myth We need to buy a particular catalogue product.

    Fact No. The policy does not name a product. Use what you have. Policy

  • Myth The return needs the names of our data owners.

    Fact No. It asks only whether each role is assigned. Keep names in your own records. GDS position

  • Myth SECRET data has to be listed somewhere.

    Fact No. Only OFFICIAL data is in scope. Policy

  • Myth Anything not fully in place means we fail.

    Fact No. The first return sets a baseline to improve from. Report your real position, including what is planned or in development. GDS position

Source: Data Asset Management Policy in government (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

What GDS offers

  • Published guidance and standards, including the data quality action plan guide and the data quality issues framework.
  • Support targeted at organisations' different levels of data maturity and resources, starting with assets that are proven to be reused.
  • A service, being developed, to make data asset metadata easy to find across government.
