Make the policy part of how you work

The return is one moment in the year. The policy works when knowing, owning and looking after your shared data becomes normal, both in day-to-day business and whenever something changes.

The yearly cycle

Each stage comes from a requirement in the policy. Policy

  1. 1IdentifyTest possible assets against the scope principles.
  2. 2RecordDescribe each asset in your catalogue.
  3. 3OwnName an owner, and a steward to look after it.
  4. 4Assure qualityKeep a quality plan, and act on the issues it finds.
  5. 5ReportSend your return to GDS.
  6. 6ReviewLook at the whole list again at least once a year.

Source: Data Asset Management Policy in government (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab)

In business as usual

Ways organisations can keep the policy working after the first return. Suggested practice

  • Put it in role descriptions

    Write data owner and steward duties into the roles that hold them, so they survive people moving on.

  • Use your existing governance

    Take the asset list and quality plans to the board or committee that already oversees information risk.

  • Fix a yearly date

    Pick a month for the annual review and put it in the business plan, ahead of the return.

  • Keep the catalogue as the source

    Update the catalogue first and fill the return from it, so you never keep two lists.

  • Track gaps, not just answers

    Keep a short list of policy gaps, such as assets with no owner, and review progress quarterly.

In change: projects and new services

Build it in from the start, so a new service's data joins your list without extra work. This follows the Service Standard's delivery phases and point 10 of the Technology Code of Practice, which asks teams to plan for data across its lifecycle. Suggested practice

  1. Discovery

    List the data the service will create, use or share with other organisations. Note which critical purposes it serves.

  2. Alpha

    Run each likely asset through the asset test. Agree who will own it. Check the legal basis for any sharing.

  3. Beta

    Describe each asset in your catalogue using the core metadata. Start its data quality action plan.

  4. Live

    Make sure the assets are in your organisation's next return, and hand ownership to the business, not the project.

  5. Retirement

    When the service closes, update or remove its assets from your list and your catalogue.

Source: Technology Code of Practice, point 10: make better use of data (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); Service Standard (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab)
