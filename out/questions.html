Scope and critical data assets

Which organisations does the policy apply to? Policy

All UK government departments and their arm's length bodies: executive agencies, non-departmental public bodies and non-ministerial departments. It is optional for public corporations and for local government. The devolved governments use their own data sharing governance, though the principles are useful to anyone.

Source: Data Asset Management Policy in government (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Exactly what is in scope? (the four scope principles) GDS position asked 1×

An asset is in scope only if it is OFFICIAL and all four principles apply together:

  1. It meets a critical criterion: it supports at least one of the seven critical purposes.
  2. It is shared: it has value across government through reuse. It is shared with a public sector organisation under a data sharing agreement, published as open data, or could create value if shared.
  3. You are accountable for it: your organisation manages or controls it, including data from others that you have materially changed or enriched.
  4. It is the source: if an extract, report, API or product is critical, report the source data behind it. Report a product separately only if it is managed and owned in its own right.

The principles were developed with the DAM Policy Working Group. They put the published guidance into practice, so use them where the template's wording seems to differ. Worked scenarios are being added.

Test an asset against the principles.

Source: Identifying and recording critical data assets (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Answers needs N02 below.

Does data we only use inside our organisation count? GDS position asked 1×

Not on its own. The policy is about data with value across government. Some data is critical inside your organisation, such as some workforce and finance data, but has no value across government, so it is out of scope. An extract of it that is shared for analysis may be in scope.

GDS recommends labelling assets that are in scope as XG_CDA in your data catalogue, so they are easy to tell apart.

Source: Identifying and recording critical data assets (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Answers needs N01 below.

How do we judge if data could create value if shared? GDS position

It is a judgement, so be practical and do not hold up your return. For the Baseline Return, start with assets already shared under a data sharing agreement or published as open data. If you have time, add assets that could create value if they were easier to find and use. Signs of that include data that:

  • supports needs that many organisations have
  • is about common things such as people, places, organisations, businesses or services
  • another organisation has asked for
  • matters nationally, regionally or across sectors

More guidance will follow after January 2027, ready for the October 2027 return.

Source: Identifying and recording critical data assets (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Are official statistics, or our extracts of another department's statistics, in scope? Policy asked 1×

Do not list published official statistics themselves. List the data behind them: the administrative data, surveys and censuses. That data is listed by the organisation that is accountable for it.

An extract you take from another department is yours to list only if you have materially changed or enriched it. Otherwise the source belongs to the other department.

Source: Identifying and recording critical data assets (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Answers needs N03 below.

Do we report SECRET or TOP SECRET data? Policy asked 1×

No. Only OFFICIAL data is in scope, including OFFICIAL with markings and descriptors such as sensitive. SECRET and TOP SECRET data is excluded.

Listing an asset does not force you to share it. The policy does not create a duty to share data or remove any legal obligation.

Source: Data Asset Management Policy in government (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Answers needs N04 below.

How do Essential Shared Data Assets (ESDAs) relate to critical data assets? GDS position

The DAM Policy grew out of the Essential Shared Data Assets policy. The name changed to critical data asset, but the definition is the same. The ESDA exercise was a test using a sample of assets. The DAM Policy applies across all of government.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

We already have our own definition of critical data assets. What do we do? GDS position

Keep it for your own purposes. The DAM Policy definition covers data with value across government, so some assets you treat as critical will not be in scope. How you manage both is up to you. To keep catalogues consistent across government, label the ones in scope of the policy XG_CDA.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

We have not formally identified our critical data assets yet. What now? GDS position

Make a reasonable effort to identify and report them in line with the policy and guidance. The first return sets a baseline to improve from. If you cannot meet a deadline, tell GDS through your lead coordinator.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Do we report all our data assets, or only critical ones? GDS position

Only critical data assets as the policy defines them. You can catalogue anything else for your own purposes, but it does not go in the return.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Roles and governance

Who is accountable, and who should our coordinator be? Policy asked 1×

Your chief data officer is responsible for putting the policy into practice. If you have no chief data officer, the director responsible for data is accountable. Every asset must have a named data owner.

The coordinator role comes from the programme, not the policy. Your chief data officer names someone who knows your organisation's data and gives them time for the work. See who does what.

Source: Data Asset Management Policy in government (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); Data ownership model (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Answers needs N10 below.

Does the data owner role replace information asset owners? Policy

No. Information asset owners keep their role and their accountability for managing, securing and complying with rules about information assets. The data owner role adds to it by making clear who is accountable for how data is accessed, used, improved and governed. The data ownership model explains the difference. Training for data owners is being developed.

Source: Data ownership model (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Who reports an asset when ownership is shared or spread across teams? GDS position

The organisation that holds data ownership accountability reports it. Where there is a shared service, agree between you which organisation that is. Others may have stewardship or operational roles, but each asset has one named data owner.

Source: Data ownership model (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Data management requirements

What metadata is mandatory? Policy asked 1×

The minimum set is in Recording metadata to describe critical data assets. It recommends the Data Catalogue Vocabulary (DCAT) to describe data sets and data services. The UK Cross-Government Metadata Exchange Model is one practical way to apply it.

The return asks for less than this: a smaller set of details GDS uses to monitor the policy. Keep the full metadata in your own catalogue. See step 7.

Source: Recording metadata to describe critical data assets (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Answers needs N11 below.

Can we keep richer metadata than the minimum? GDS position

Yes. The policy sets a minimum. Keep any extra metadata you need, and keep meeting any higher standards that already apply to you, such as internal rules, regulation or security policies.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

How does a data catalogue relate to our information asset register? GDS position

They overlap but do different jobs. An information asset register is mainly for governance: accountability, risk, security and compliance. A data catalogue helps people find, understand and use data, with richer details such as quality and lineage. A catalogue could replace a register only if it does the governance job as well. Avoid keeping the same details twice: agree one source for the shared fields and link the two.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Do we need a particular data catalogue product? Policy asked 5×

No. The policy asks you to manage metadata about your assets in your data catalogue and report it to GDS. It does not name a product. GDS ran a catalogue pilot on Microsoft Purview because many organisations already use it, and would repeat the pilot with other tools if there is demand. Peers share how they use their tools at the Data Catalogue and Analysis Platform Working Group.

Source: Data Asset Management Policy in government (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Answers needs N12 below.

What counts as a data quality action plan "or equivalent"? GDS position asked 3×

Every asset must have a data quality action plan or an equivalent. To count as equivalent, your own process must cover all seven steps of the GDS guide:

  1. Identify critical data
  2. Identify data quality rules
  3. Assess current data quality
  4. Prioritise improvements and set goals
  5. Find the root cause and act
  6. Report on data quality
  7. Measure again over time

The policy does not require a particular document or tool. GDS is building a bank of examples.

Source: Data Asset Management Policy in government (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); Data quality action plan: implementation guide (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); Data quality issues framework (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); The Government Data Quality Framework (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Implementation and support

This is a big task. Do we absorb it or set up a team? GDS position

GDS is not prescriptive about how you organise the work, because organisations differ so much. It will work through the challenges with lead coordinators in the DAM Policy Working Group, and share how others are approaching it.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Are the timings for prioritised support fixed? GDS position

No, they are indicative. They are based on how complex and widely used each department's data was in the ESDA returns. If the timing does not suit your capacity, or you need closer support, raise it through your lead coordinator.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Can we get feedback on a test return? Suggested practice asked 3×

Yes. First run your list through Is my list ready to send?. It runs in your browser and finds the most common problems. Then ask for feedback through your lead coordinator or the contact form (opens in a new tab). Do not attach the file to the form.

Reporting and the return

When are the returns due? GDS position asked 2×

The first return, the Baseline Return, is due in January 2027. The next is due in October 2027. Aim to be ready by Friday 18 December 2026, before the Christmas break.

The policy itself asks you to maintain and review your list every year. In time, GDS expects to replace these returns with more frequent updates taken straight from your catalogue.

Source: Data Asset Management Policy in government (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Answers needs N08 below.

Do arm's length bodies send their own return, or one with their department? GDS position asked 3×

Arm's length bodies identify and record their own critical data assets using the return template. They send it to their parent department first, and the department forwards it to GDS. Departments coordinate and assure their arm's length bodies' returns, and send a combined return where that makes sense. Either one combined return or separate returns is fine, as long as the department knows about every one.

If your arm's length body has not heard from its department about the return, contact GDS.

Source: Data Asset Management Policy in government (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Answers needs N06 below.

Does the return assess compliance or maturity? GDS position

Compliance with the DAM Policy. The Baseline Return shows which assets are in scope and how far they meet the data management requirements. Progress is reported to the Chief Data Officer Council and to ministers after each return. Data maturity is assessed separately, by the GDS Data Maturity Service.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Do we give the names of data owners and stewards? GDS position

No. The return asks only whether each role has been assigned. Do not enter anyone's name. Keep names in your own catalogue or register.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

What evidence should we keep? GDS position

You do not send evidence with the return. Be able to trace each answer back to the system it came from, such as your data catalogue or information asset register. Give each row a unique identifier, so later returns can show what is new or changed.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

How do we report something that is only partly in place? GDS position

Choose the option in the template that matches where you really are. Use In development when work has started but is not finished, and Planned or No when it has not started. Do not overstate. For data quality, say whether you have a standard plan, a documented equivalent, or nothing in place yet.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

How much should we write in free-text fields? GDS position

Keep it short. Use free text only for context the options cannot give: exceptions, partial progress, dependencies, risks, next steps or a non-sensitive evidence reference. Never include personal data, sensitive information, detailed quality scores or rules.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

What goes in the legal gateway field? GDS position asked 1×

The law or agreement that allows the data to be shared or made available, not the basis on which you first collected it. If more than one applies, give the main one if you know it. If there is no clear main one, leave the field blank. You do not need to review several data sharing agreements to fill it in. Recording legal gateways in your catalogue is still good practice.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Answers needs N09 below.

How do we answer the discoverability and interoperability field? GDS position

It asks whether information about the data standards the asset uses can be found, inside your organisation and across government, for example on data.gov.uk or a sector portal.

  • In place: full details are available on every standard used.
  • Partial: some details, but not complete.
  • Not in place: no details are available.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Do we report data sets, data products or APIs? GDS position asked 1×

Report the underlying source data set. Where a critical product, report or API draws on data sets, name those source data sets. You may add details of APIs if you already have them, but you do not need to look for them. If one data set is reached through several APIs, report the data set once.

The policy defines a data asset as one or more data sets plus any data services, such as APIs, used to reach them.

Source: Data Asset Management Policy in government (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Answers needs N05 below.

Will other departments see the metadata we send? GDS position asked 2×

Not for now. Returns go to GDS. Sharing metadata across government is being considered as part of the GDS discoverability service, and members will be able to help shape it. The policy says GDS is developing that service.

Source: Data Asset Management Policy in government (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Answers needs N13 below.

How will changes to the template be managed? GDS position

The template for the Baseline Return is agreed. A change log starts when the first returns arrive. Unique identifiers on each row let GDS see what is new or changed between returns.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Related initiatives

How does this relate to the National Data Library? GDS position

The two work closely together to avoid duplication. The National Data Library is focused on developing data.gov.uk. The DAM Policy makes sure good data management practice is applied to government data, which makes it more valuable to initiatives like the library.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

How does this relate to HM Treasury's Strategic Asset Review? GDS position

They ask different things. The Strategic Asset Review looks for assets of all kinds with high commercial potential. The DAM Policy is about critical data that is shared, and commercialisation is not its aim. Some assets may appear in both, but the returns will not be the same.

Source: GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

How does this relate to the Metadata Exchange Model? GDS position

The metadata guidance recommends DCAT for describing data sets and data services. The UK Cross-Government Metadata Exchange Model is a practical way to apply DCAT, and can help you record and exchange metadata consistently.

Source: Recording metadata to describe critical data assets (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); GDS DAM Policy FAQs, November 2026

Embedding the policy

How do we build the policy into a new service or project? Suggested practice asked 1×

Treat it as part of the design, not a task at the end. During discovery, name the data the service will create, use or share. Before live, give each critical asset an owner, describe it in your catalogue and start a quality plan, so it joins your next return without extra work. See embedding it in change.

This matches point 10 of the Technology Code of Practice, which asks teams to plan for data across its whole lifecycle.

Source: Technology Code of Practice, point 10: make better use of data (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); Data Asset Management Policy in government (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab)

Answers needs N15 below.

How does the policy link to resilience data sharing? Policy asked 1×

Defence, national security and resilience is one of the seven critical purposes. Data shared for resilience work can be a critical data asset if the other principles apply. Listing it records that it exists and who owns it. It does not change who may use it.

Source: Identifying and recording critical data assets (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab); Data Asset Management Policy in government (GOV.UK) (opens in a new tab)
